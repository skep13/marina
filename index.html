01

Not a chat window

Marina runs in a frameless, always-on-top window with no background — she stands on your desktop, not inside an app. You reach her by typing or by holding a key and speaking. Speech recognition, voice synthesis and rendering all happen locally; only the language model sits behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, so it can be Ollama on the same laptop, a box on your LAN, or a hosted API without anything else changing.

She is written as a friend rather than an assistant. She has opinions, a TikTok account she complains about, and permission to be busy, unimpressed, or simply unhelpful.

02

How she moves

Three problems worth solving properly rather than faking.

−46 dB → −14 dB

A mouth, not a hinge

Loudness in decibels sets how far the jaw opens. The balance between the first two formant bands picks between five visemes, so the mouth changes shape rather than just size. Sibilants are detected and damped — otherwise every "s" reads as a shout.

aa · ih · ou · ee · oh
140 joints · 49 mm travel

Hair with real physics

The VRM already carries spring-bone simulation; it just has nothing to react to when she stands still. Instead of animating strands, a simulated breeze leans the gravity direction each joint falls toward — so the solver does the work, and long strands swing further than short ones for free.

non-harmonic gusts, so it never loops audibly
19 cues

Performed, not narrated

When the model writes *tilts head*, that is stripped from the speech and played as an animation instead of read aloud as "asterisk tilts head asterisk". Each cue fires at the point in the audio where it was written, not dumped at the start.

nod · shake · shrug · eyeroll · wink · sigh · …
03

Her voice

Kokoro, 82M parameters, running on the laptop's CPU at roughly three times realtime. Two voicepacks blended for timbre, then pitched up a semitone without changing duration — generation is slowed by the same ratio the resample speeds it back up by.
